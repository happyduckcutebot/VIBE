What VIBE is about

1. Start with curiosity
2. Use AI to build

4. Show real results
What is the VIBE Club?

A student-led applied AI and business technology community.

VIBE is not a traditional coding club. It is a low-entry learning community where curiosity matters more than prior coding experience.

AI-assisted problem solving

A place where business students use AI-assisted coding to solve real problems in business, finance, entrepreneurship, marketing, operations, and analytics.

Low-entry learning

Students do not need to arrive as expert programmers. The club provides a supportive environment for asking basic questions, experimenting, and learning with peers.

Project platform

VIBE supports websites, apps, dashboards, data tools, trading bots, AI workflows, and prototypes that students can show in interviews and internships.

Potential activities

Build ideas, solve real needs, and create useful tools.

VIBE can support member-driven projects, real-world collaborations, Opus and student club projects, finance and data work, and a living dataset on AI adoption.

💡

Build your own ideas

Members can bring ideas for apps, websites, dashboards, trading bots, data tools, or AI workflows. VIBE helps students find teammates with complementary skills in business, design, coding, data, and communication.

  • Low-pressure space to test ideas before they are polished.
  • Project scoping, starter templates, peer feedback, and faculty guidance.
  • Portfolio-ready outputs for interviews and internships.
  • Paid AI tools and hardware.
  • Guest speakers from related industries.

Student question: What have you wanted to build but did not know where to start?

🤝

Real-world collaborations

VIBE members can help solve real needs for firms, campus groups, alumni, entrepreneurs, nonprofits, and local businesses.

  • From firms: automate parts of equity research workflows, scrape public industry sales data or government data, and build dashboards for recurring business questions.
  • From campus: support departments, faculty, and student clubs with websites and tools; help UBC pitch competition winners turn ideas into apps, websites, or prototypes; analyze survey, event, or student opportunity data.
  • From community: work with alumni, entrepreneurs, nonprofits, and local businesses; translate messy business needs into manageable student projects; build professional habits: scope, communicate, deliver, and document.
🏫

Build for Opus and student clubs

VIBE can interact with many clubs and programs instead of operating in isolation.

  • Build websites for business school events, speaker series, and student programs.
  • Create reusable website templates for clubs and competitions.
  • Develop dashboards for events, opportunities, alumni speakers, and student resources.
  • Use AI to draft event recaps, newsletter blurbs, and social media content for review.
  • Partner with UBC and pitch competitions to help selected winners make ideas more concrete.
  • Support student founders with app prototypes, landing pages, surveys, and simulations.
  • Work with Finance Club, Scholars of Finance, marketing clubs, and analytics groups on shared projects.
  • Invite clubs to bring problems that VIBE students can help solve.
📈

Finance, data, and trading projects

Project ideas can combine business judgment with technical tools.

  • Equity research automation: scrape public industry sales data, pull company filings and financial statements, build dashboards for comparable firms and valuation ratios, and generate first-draft research summaries for human review.
  • SEC EDGAR projects: use free EDGAR data to scrape 10-K and 10-Q filings; track AI mentions, cybersecurity disclosures, risk factors, or executive compensation; build datasets for class projects or student research.
  • Trading bots and market data: study technical trading bots students are already using or curious about; build backtesting bots for momentum, moving averages, mean reversion, and pairs trading; evaluate performance, risk, and transaction costs.
  • For selected projects, use proprietary high-frequency trading data from Prof. Zhang’s research.
