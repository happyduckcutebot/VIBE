What is the VIBE Club?

A student-led applied AI and business technology community.

VIBE is not a coding club.

  • A place where business students use AI-assisted coding to solve real problems.
  • A low-entry learning community: curiosity matters more than prior coding experience.
  • A project platform for websites, apps, dashboards, data tools, trading bots, and prototypes.

Core idea

Business students can become builders — not just users — when AI lowers the coding barrier.

The club helps students turn business questions into working tools.

Why Do We Need a Student Club?

Because students need a supportive bridge from interest to execution.

1

The challenge

  • AI is changing business work quickly.
  • Many students want to learn, but coding feels intimidating.
  • Class time alone is not enough for open-ended projects.
2

The support

  • Peer learning and student leadership.
  • Beginner-friendly workshops using AI tools.
  • A safe place to ask “basic” questions and experiment.
  • Funding support for pro subscriptions and hardware.
3

The bridge

  • Turn ideas into usable websites, apps, dashboards, and data tools.
  • Connect student clubs, entrepreneurs, faculty, alumni, and firms.
  • Create visible results students can talk about and show.

Finance, Data, and Trading Projects

Project ideas that combine business judgment with technical tools.

Equity research automation

  • Scrape public industry sales data for research inputs.
  • Pull company filings and financial statements.
  • Build dashboards for comparable firms and valuation ratios.
  • Generate first-draft research summaries for human review.

SEC EDGAR projects

  • Use free EDGAR data to scrape 10-K and 10-Q filings.
  • Track AI mentions, cybersecurity disclosures, risk factors, or executive compensation.
  • Build datasets that can support class projects or student research.

Trading bots & market data

  • Study technical trading bots students are already using or curious about.
  • Build backtesting bots: momentum, moving averages, mean reversion, pairs trading.
  • Use market and transaction data to evaluate performance, risk, and transaction costs.
  • For selected projects, use proprietary high-frequency trading data from my research.
